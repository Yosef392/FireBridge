NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots

FireBridge, explained

Two NASA satellites count fires differently, so the 25-year fire record has a fake jump in 2012. FireBridge learns the difference from the years both sensors overlap and rebuilds the older years on the newer sensor's scale, giving one consistent record for eight very different fire regions.

01

The problem: a jump that never happened

NASA's MODIS instruments (on Terra and Aqua) have recorded active fires since November 2000. VIIRS on Suomi NPP joined in 2012 with pixels about seven times smaller in area. A fire MODIS records as one hot pixel can show up as several VIIRS pixels, and small fires MODIS misses entirely become visible.

Join the two records naively, MODIS until 2012 and VIIRS after, and fire activity seems to multiply overnight. In the Nile Delta the raw record jumps 9.4×, while the real change, measured by MODIS alone on both sides, is 1.2×. Anyone reading trends from the joined record, whether a scientist, a land manager or an emergency planner, would be misled.

02

Data and study regions

All data comes from NASA FIRMS, which publishes yearly active-fire CSV files per country and sensor. Each row is one hot pixel: position, date and time, satellite, brightness temperatures, fire radiative power (FRP, MW), pixel size (scan × track km), confidence, day/night and a type code.

Cleaning rules the data taught us

Vegetation fires only. FIRMS type 2 marks static industrial heat, mostly gas flares: 68.6% of Egypt's VIIRS rows and 31.0% of its MODIS rows. We keep type 0 only. The ratio is not constant: in the Nile Delta VIIRS records 7 to 21 detections per MODIS detection depending on the year. Aqua and Suomi NPP fly together: the median gap between an Aqua detection and VIIRS seeing the same fire is about 22 minutes, while Terra passes about 3 hours earlier.

Eight regions, eight fire types

The team's dataset plan picks regions with very different fires, each with a documented event that serves as a check. FIRMS only offers whole-country files, so each country is downloaded and cut to the region's rectangle.

03

The pipeline: five notebooks to a live app

Engineering decisions that mattered

Parquet + DuckDB. Loading every country into pandas crashed Colab's memory. Each file is now converted on its own with DuckDB (38 MB of Egypt CSVs became 2.9 MB of Parquet) and analysis queries only what it needs. Retry on false "not found". FIRMS sometimes answers 404 for files that exist, which silently dropped years; downloads now retry three times and the listing reports any missing years. Reproducibility. DuckDB returns rows in no fixed order, which changed model results between runs; tables are sorted before training and every model has a fixed seed. Two bugs found and fixed: region names were compared in lowercase, which zeroed the region features, and an early run included whole countries that contain the study regions, which double-counted fires.

04

Version 1: converting detections one by one

The first approach asked: for one MODIS detection, how many VIIRS detections did the same fire produce? Aqua detections were matched with VIIRS detections within ±50 minutes and inside the MODIS pixel's own footprint plus 0.5 km, each VIIRS detection counted once for its nearest MODIS pixel. Seven models were compared, from the average ratio to XGBoost and a small neural network with Poisson loss; on Egypt and Portugal, XGBoost reached a deviance of 1.62 against 2.78 for the ratio.

It still could not work as a harmonization, for three reasons:

Fire type matters

Trained only on Portugal, the model overpredicted Egypt by 84%; trained only on Egypt, it underpredicted Portugal by 71%.

Terra is left out

Matching needs Aqua's timing, but before mid-2002 only Terra exists.

The lesson

Model totals per area and month, using every MODIS detection from both satellites as input. That is version 2.

05

Version 2: harmonization per 10 km cell and month

Every detection is placed on an equal-area grid (EPSG:6933, EASE-Grid 2.0) of 10 × 10 km cells, so each cell covers the same ground at every latitude. For every cell that ever burned and every month from 2000 to 2025, the model predicts how many detections VIIRS would have made, using only what MODIS saw. Months with no MODIS detection are kept: they are how the model learns the blind spot.

x = R · k₀ · λ y = R · sin φ / k₀ R = 6,371,007.181 m, k₀ = cos 30° cell = ( ⌊x / 10 000⌋ , ⌊y / 10 000⌋ )

The main model: a ratio times a learned correction

b = r_a · m + 1[m = 0] · z_a + 0.001 log μ = log b + f(x) loss = Σ ( μ − y · log μ ) (Poisson)

Three models are compared. A: the region's average VIIRS/MODIS ratio. B: plain gradient boosting with Poisson loss, which fits cells well but cannot go far beyond the largest values it saw. C: the ratio × a learned correction. Settings for B and C: 800 trees, learning rate 0.05, depth 6, min child weight 5, 80% row and feature sampling, L2 = 2, on a GPU.

From predictions to a calendar

Five copies of model C, each trained on a bootstrap resample of 2013–2025, predict every cell-month. Their mean is the harmonized value; the 10th–90th percentile of their regional sums is the band. The calendar shows real VIIRS from 2013 and the model before. Two checks turn it into evidence: the 2012 jump check (VIIRS 2013–15 ÷ harmonized 2009–11 should match MODIS 2013–15 ÷ MODIS 2009–11), and anomaly z-scores of each month against the same month in other years, leaving the known event year out of the baseline.

06

The numbers

The split is by year, never random, so one fire never lands in both training and test. Models are compared on 2021 and scored once on 2022–2025. The current year, 2026, will be harmonized and shown but never scored.

How we measure

Cell deviance: Poisson deviance per 10 km cell-month, the fit the map depends on (lower is better). Total bias: how far the summed count is off. Monthly WAPE: the typical miss on a region's monthly total, as a share of the real count. Monthly correlation: whether the ups and downs match.

09

What can be improved

The challenge asks for a harmonized MODIS–VIIRS hot-spot record and a burning-activity calendar that responders, scientists and land managers can trust. Measured against those goals, here is where FireBridge stands and what would move it furthest. Priority reflects impact on those goals against effort.

A harmonized record

Done for counts in 8 regions; jump removed everywhere. Gaps: FRP is not harmonized, NOAA-20/21 unused.

A burning-activity calendar

Done per region, monthly, 2001–2025; 2026 being added. Gaps: fixed regions only, no season-onset or trend statistics.

Something users can trust

Held-out tests and event checks done. Gaps: the band is too narrow, no independent ground truth, weak on unseen fire types.

Make the harmonization more accurate

HighTrust

Calibrate the uncertainty band

The band shows only how much five models disagree, so it is far narrower than the real 11.1% error. Replace it with conformal or quantile intervals calibrated on held-out years, and report how often the truth falls inside.

HighRecord

Remove the floor in quiet months

Where MODIS saw nothing, the model starts from the region's average blind-spot count, so it rarely predicts zero and quiet months before 2013 look busier than after (Canada's winters). A two-part hurdle model, first "any fire?" then "how many?", would fix this.

HighTrust

Handle new fire types

Held out, the Nile Delta is −52% and Portugal +76%. Swap the 7 region flags for descriptors that exist anywhere (land cover from MCD12Q1, typical fire size, cropland share, climate zone) and add more regions such as Punjab and California so the model learns fire types, not region names.

MediumRecord

Get the extremes right

Record months are still low (Portugal, September 2024: about 4,000 predicted vs 5,765 real). Weight large months more, try a Tweedie or tail-aware loss, and test specifically on the top 1% of months.

MediumRecord

Harmonize fire radiative power too

Counts depend on pixel size; FRP measures energy and is closer to what responders and emission scientists need. Apply the same cell-month approach to FRP sums and compare with published MODIS–VIIRS FRP calibrations.

MediumRecord

Use every VIIRS satellite

NOAA-20 (2018) and NOAA-21 (2023) carry VIIRS too. Adding them gives more overpasses and a cross-check of the target itself.

LowerTrust

Test the Terra-only years

2000–2002 rely on Terra alone. Simulate them in the overlap years by hiding Aqua and measuring the error, and check whether Terra and Aqua's late-life orbit drift changes the learned relationship.

LowerTrust

Explain the corrections

Export SHAP values or gain importance from every run, so users can see why a cell was scaled up, for example by its burning neighbours.

Make the calendar more useful

HighTrust

Validate against independent records

Compare the harmonized record with burned-area products (MODIS MCD64A1, VIIRS burned area) and national fire statistics (Portugal ICNF, Canada CWFIS). Agreement there is stronger evidence than agreement with VIIRS alone.

HighCalendar

Any place on Earth

Run the pipeline globally on a 0.25° grid and let users draw their own area, instead of eight fixed rectangles that also include slices of neighbouring land.

MediumCalendar

Season and trend statistics

Report fire-season start, peak and length per year, and test long-term trends (Mann–Kendall, Sen's slope) on the harmonized record, which is only possible now that the 2012 jump is gone.

MediumUsers

Keep it current

Schedule a monthly job that pulls new FIRMS data, appends it, and flags months that are already unusual against the 25-year baseline: an early signal for responders.

MediumUsers

Let people take the data

CSV and NetCDF downloads of the harmonized record with its band, plus a small API, so scientists can use it in their own analyses.

LowerUsers

Reach more users

Arabic and other languages, a simpler mobile view, and short guidance on reading model estimates versus observations.
